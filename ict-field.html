ICT Field

My brother works as a software engineer at an international company. He's 16 years older than me and taught himself JavaScript back when he finished his WO bachelor's in European studies, which is completely different to what he does now.

Because he started working in the field around 11 years ago, he is considered a senior and will always be able to find a job. Just like my father who worked for over 15 years as a programmer would also still be able to get a job in the IT field.

Even though my father's knowledge about the current ways (like new programming languages) is limited, because he has a substantial knowledge about Systems Engineering in general, he can easily apply that knowledge when for example working with AI.

My brother always talks about how the field has changed in the past few years, and how he went from actually producing code to being more of an architect/manager of a bunch of agents that produce code for him. He went from the writer to the builder, and simply writing code is something he misses.

These developments have made me think more carefully about the direction I want to take during my HBO-ICT studies.

Although my main interest lies in Data Science, I believe that understanding the bigger picture of IT is becoming increasingly important. Being able to write code and develop models is valuable, but so is understanding how different technologies work together to solve a client's problem.

This is why I do consider a minor in Systems Engineering, although I also saw in the curriculum of year two that some things already get covered. Either way, I want to learn more about the process of translating a client's requirements into a functional product, from analyzing the initial problem and designing a solution to integrating different components and evaluating the result.

Because the IT field is constantly changing, specific tools and programming languages may become less relevant over time. But the ability to understand complex systems, solve problems, and adapt to new technologies remains valuable.

I don't see AI as a reason to stop learning how to program. Instead, I see it as another reason to develop a strong technical foundation while also learning how to design and manage larger systems. Because I want to become someone who doesn't just know how to build a solution, but also understands what needs to be built, why it matters, and how to make it work in practice.
